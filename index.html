03 / Honors Research

From prototype to research.

Honors Thesis · 2026

Theoretical Feasibility Evaluation of LoRa Communication for Wildfire Detection Networks

My Honors thesis extended the wildfire-detection capstone by evaluating whether LoRa communication could realistically support a distributed wildfire sensor network beyond the conditions demonstrated during prototype testing.

The analysis focused on communication range, packet reliability, latency, environmental propagation loss, relay behavior, and sensor-node energy consumption.

Read Honors Thesis
Range

Vegetation, terrain, environmental loss, and node placement can substantially change real-world LoRa performance.

Reliability

End-to-end delivery probability decreases as additional relay links are added.

Latency

The roughly 60-second reporting interval affects response time far more than LoRa packet airtime.

Energy

The MQ-2 heater dominates node power consumption while LoRa transmission contributes comparatively little average power.

Presented: August 26, 2026
