01 / CAPSTONE · FEATURED

LoRa wildfire detection network

Designed and built a multi-node environmental sensing network intended to explore low-cost, long-range wildfire detection in areas where cellular or Wi-Fi coverage may be limited.

  • Integrated ESP32 controllers with RFM95W LoRa radios, BME280 environmental sensing, and MQ-2 smoke/gas sensing.
  • Implemented a transmitter → relay → receiver architecture with node IDs, packet forwarding, and a TTL-based multi-hop scheme.
  • Worked on PCB/system integration, sensor validation, enclosure considerations, and field testing for range, RSSI/SNR, reliability, and latency.
  • Evaluated how terrain, node placement, radio settings, and relay behavior affect practical deployment.
Result: Demonstrated at least 500 m communication range, approximately 97% packet delivery, and sub-5-second network latency. The project received Oregon State’s Environmental Impact Award.

02 / HONORS RESEARCH · FEATURED

LoRa wildfire network feasibility analysis

Extended the capstone into an Honors thesis that asked whether the demonstrated prototype could scale into a technically defensible wildfire-monitoring network.

  • Built a theoretical link-budget and propagation analysis around the capstone’s 915 MHz operating point.
  • Modeled packet reliability across relay paths to understand how per-link PDR compounds across multiple hops.
  • Analyzed latency and reporting intervals for a network transmitting roughly once per minute.
  • Estimated node energy consumption and showed that the MQ-2 heater, rather than LoRa transmission, dominates the power budget.
Focus: Reach, reliability, responsiveness, and energy — translating a successful prototype into deployment-level engineering tradeoffs.
Link budget Reliability modeling Latency Power analysis
03 / ANALOG IC DESIGN · FEATURED

Two-stage CMOS operational amplifier

Designed and simulated a two-stage operational amplifier at transistor level, balancing gain, bandwidth, output swing, and closed-loop stability.

  • Sized MOSFET devices and biasing for the input differential stage and gain stage.
  • Used Miller compensation to improve phase margin and stabilize the two-stage topology.
  • Ran DC, AC, transient, and operating-point simulations to evaluate performance and design tradeoffs.
  • Iterated device sizing and compensation based on simulated gain, bandwidth, and stability behavior.
Tools: Cadence Virtuoso · 0.18 µm CMOS · transistor-level simulation
Cadence Virtuoso CMOS Miller compensation Analog design
04 / RF & ANTENNAS · FEATURED

Broadside collinear dipole array

Modeled a multi-element antenna array intended to produce broadside radiation suitable for cellular-style coverage.

  • Used MATLAB to study array-factor behavior, element spacing, directivity, and sidelobe tradeoffs.
  • Built and simulated the antenna geometry in HFSS to compare full-wave behavior with analytical expectations.
  • Evaluated how spacing and array configuration influence the resulting radiation pattern.
  • Compared modeled performance and design assumptions to understand practical RF constraints.
Focus: Radiation patterns · array spacing · directivity · simulation-to-theory comparison
