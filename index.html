03 / Honors Research

Honors Thesis · 2026

Theoretical Feasibility Evaluation of LoRa Communication for Wildfire Detection Networks

My Honors thesis extended the wildfire-detection capstone by examining whether LoRa communication could realistically support a distributed wildfire sensor network beyond the conditions demonstrated during prototype testing.

The analysis focused on communication range, packet reliability, latency, environmental propagation loss, relay behavior, and sensor node energy consumption.

Read Honors Thesis

Key findings

  1. Range — Actual field performance can vary significantly with vegetation, terrain, node placement, and environmental loss.
  2. Reliability — End-to-end delivery decreases as additional independent relay links are added to the communication path.
  3. Latency — The approximately 60-second reporting interval has a much greater impact on response time than LoRa packet airtime.
  4. Energy — The MQ-2 sensor heater dominates the sensor-node power requirement, while LoRa transmission contributes comparatively little average power.
Presented: August 26, 2026
